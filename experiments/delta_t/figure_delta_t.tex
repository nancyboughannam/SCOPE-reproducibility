\textcolor{red}{
We evaluate the sensitivity of the final controller to planning intervals
of 15, 30, 60, and 90~s, using ten additional paired iterations
(3601--3610). The predictors trained using 60-second windows, the candidate
catalogue, $\lambda=0.01$, and $R_{\max}=0.15$ are held fixed. The
steady-workload cases use 900 and 1800 vehicles. In the 1200-vehicle
workload-shift case, the task-arrival rate is multiplied by 1.5 from
1200 to 2400~s and then returns to its initial level. Each simulation
lasts 60~min, including a 3-minute warm-up.
The implementation resets its telemetry counters at each planning epoch;
changing $\Delta T$ therefore changes both decision frequency and the
measurement window supplied to the fixed predictors. The reconfiguration
bound and penalty remain defined per update. This experiment assesses
sensitivity of the deployed controller, rather than comparing separately
retrained models or establishing a universally optimal planning interval.
The main comparative study retains its preselected interval of 60~s.
}
